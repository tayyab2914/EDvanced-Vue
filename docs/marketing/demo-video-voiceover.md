# EDvanced Vue — Voiceover Recording Script

**For:** the narrator (or the TTS voice) recording the 90-second demo.
**Copy status:** the client's narration, **word for word**. Nothing added, nothing cut.
**Target:** 80–90 seconds. Lands at **1:29.4** read at 150 wpm with the pauses below honoured.
**Tone:** conversational, confident, professional. A colleague explaining something useful — not an
announcer selling something.

Companion document: [demo-video-script.md](./demo-video-script.md) — the picture, shot by shot,
already timed against this read.

---

## 1. Clean read

*The booth copy. Print this page alone for the narrator's stand — direction lives in §2.*

> Welcome to EDvanced Vue.
>
> School finance teams have plenty of reports... and plenty of data. But the challenge is being able to quickly see what that data is telling you.
>
> EDvanced Vue was created by a school finance professional with that challenge in mind — helping finance teams turn the data they already have into information they can use.
>
> When you log in, you immediately get a snapshot of your district's financial health.
>
> Projected fund balance.
>
> Days of operating cash.
>
> Revenues and expenditures.
>
> And, most importantly, areas that may need your attention.
>
> From there, you can take a deeper look. See where your revenues are coming from, compare budget to actuals, identify significant variances, and monitor how things are trending throughout the year.
>
> The same goes for expenditures, fund balance, and cash position.
>
> And instead of waiting until year-end to see where you landed... EDvanced Vue helps you monitor where you're headed — while there's still time to make decisions.
>
> Because the numbers are only part of the story.
>
> EDvanced Vue helps you see what's happening, understand what needs your attention, and tell the financial story behind the numbers.
>
> EDvanced Vue. See the story behind the numbers.

**200 words · 81.3s of speech · 8.1s of pause · 89.4s total**

## 2. Directed read

`▌` = pause, with its length. **Bold** = the word that carries the line. Timecodes are where each
line begins in the finished cut.

---

### Opening — warm, simple

**`0:00.5`** · *Warm. Unhurried. As if greeting someone who has just sat down.*

> "Welcome to EDvanced Vue."

▌ **1.0s** — hold. The logo is on screen doing nothing else. Let it be quiet.

---

**`0:03.1`** · *Plain, matter-of-fact. This is a shared observation, not a complaint.*

> "School finance teams have **plenty** of reports… ▌ **0.5s** …and **plenty** of data."

The ellipsis is a real beat, not a comma. Both "plenty"s carry equal weight — the second one is not
a punchline, it is the same word arriving again, which is the point.

**`0:08.0`** · *Slight lean-in. The problem lands here.*

> "But the challenge is being able to **quickly see** what that data is **telling** you."

Resist stressing "challenge." The interesting words are *quickly see* — that is the gap the whole
product fills.

▌ **1.2s** — the dashboard begins to appear behind. Do not rush into the next line; the picture
needs this pause to make its move.

---

### The origin — credibility

**`0:15.2`** · *This is the trust line. Slow it very slightly. Warmer than the lines around it.*

> "EDvanced Vue was created by a **school finance professional** with that challenge in mind — ▌ **0.4s** helping finance teams turn the data they **already have** into information they can **use**."

Three things to hit: *school finance professional* (who built it and why that matters), *already
have* (nothing new to collect), and *use* (the payoff). The em-dash is a short breath, not a stop.

▌ **0.5s**

---

### Executive Dashboard — slightly more energy

**`0:27.3`** · *Lift. A shade brighter and a shade faster than everything before it.*

> "When you log in, you **immediately** get a snapshot of your district's financial health."

*Immediately* is the whole sentence. Everything else is scaffolding.

---

**`0:32.9`** · *Now a list of four. Even, declarative, each one landing and stopping.*

> "Projected fund balance." ▌ **0.3s**
> "Days of operating cash." ▌ **0.3s**
> "Revenues and expenditures." ▌ **0.3s**

**Do not sing these.** No rising list-melody, no lifting on each item as though building to
something. Each is a flat statement that comes to a full stop. They are four facts a business
official reads off a screen, and the read should sound exactly that unbothered — because the
confidence comes from *not* selling them.

**`0:37.8`** · *Now break the pattern. Slower, weightier — this one matters more and should sound
like it.*

> "And, **most importantly**, areas that may need your **attention**."

The comma pair around *most importantly* is real — small beats either side. Land on *attention* and
let it sit.

▌ **0.8s**

---

### Revenue Dashboard — invitational

**`0:42.2`** · *Lighter. An offer, not an instruction.*

> "From there, you can take a **deeper** look."

---

**`0:45.4`** · *Keep momentum. Four clauses — do not let it flatten into a drone.*

> "See where your revenues are **coming from**, ▌ *(breath)* compare **budget to actuals**, ▌ *(breath)* identify **significant variances**, and monitor how things are **trending** throughout the year."

Small breaths after "coming from" and "actuals" — enough to separate the clauses, not enough to
stop. The picture highlights one card per clause, so each needs its own edge, but the sentence has
to stay one sentence.

▌ **0.4s**

---

**`0:55.4`** · *Brisk. This is a list being closed out, not opened.*

> "The same goes for **expenditures**, **fund balance**, and **cash position**."

Three quick beats. Faster than anything else in the read — the picture is cutting fast here too.

▌ **0.5s**

---

### The key message — slow here

**`0:59.9`** · *Slow down noticeably. Past tense, slightly rueful. This describes the old way.*

> "And instead of waiting until **year-end** to see where you **landed**…"

*Landed* is past and finished — say it that way, with a small downward drop. Then hold the ellipsis:

▌ **0.8s** — a real silence. The picture dead-ends on a year-end marker here. Let it.

**`1:05.5`** · *Turn. Lift on the first half, then come closer and quieter on the second.*

> "EDvanced Vue helps you monitor where you're **headed** — ▌ **0.3s** while there's **still time** to make decisions."

The single most important line in the video. *Headed* answers *landed* — same shape, opposite
direction, so give them the same weight. Then the em-dash beat, and *while there's still time* drops
in volume and moves closer to the microphone. Almost confidential. The music thins out underneath
it, so quiet reads as intimate here, not weak.

▌ **0.8s**

---

### The return — reflective

**`1:12.7`** · *Quietest line in the script. Nearly thrown away.*

> "Because the numbers are only **part** of the story."

Conversational to the point of understatement. No emphasis on "story" — that word is coming back at
the end and should not be spent here.

▌ **0.5s**

---

### Closing — confident, not salesy

**`1:16.8`** · *Three clauses that build gently. Warm, settled, in control.*

> "EDvanced Vue helps you **see** what's happening, understand what needs your **attention**, and tell the **financial story** behind the numbers."

A gentle rise across the three, landing — not spiking — on *financial story*. The temptation here is
a big finish. Do not. The client's direction is explicit: *confident, not salesy*. It should sound
like someone stating what is true, and the restraint is what makes it credible.

▌ **1.0s**

---

**`1:26.2`** · *Full stop. Assured.*

> "EDvanced Vue."

▌ **0.3s**

**`1:27.0`** · *Warm. Settled. No upward lift at the end.*

> "See the story behind the numbers."

Land it flat and finished. An upward inflection on the last word turns a statement into a pitch,
and that is precisely the thing the client asked us not to sound like.

## 4. Pronunciation and consistency

| Term | Say it | Not |
|---|---|---|
| **EDvanced** | **ED**-vanced — stress the first syllable, as in *education* | ad-VANCED, ed-VANCED |
| **Vue** | **view** — one syllable | *voo*, *vyoo-ay* |
| **EDvanced Vue** | ED-vanced **View** — five spoken syllables, no pause between the words | |

The name appears **five times**. It must sound identical every time — same stress, same pace, same
warmth. If any single take of the name is stronger than the others, use it in all five places.

Other notes:
- "Year-end" is one compound word — *YEAR-end*, not two.
- "Actuals" is the finance-industry plural noun. Natural, unremarkable, no air quotes in the voice.
- "Fund balance" and "cash position" are terms of art. Say them as familiar phrases, not as words
  being explained to someone.
- Numbers, districts and dollar figures never appear in the VO — every figure lives on screen. If a
  take drifts into ad-libbing a number, discard it.

## 5. Recording specification

**Deliver:** 48 kHz / 24-bit WAV, mono, unprocessed (no compression, EQ, or noise reduction — those
happen in the mix). One continuous take per section, not one file per line.

**Room:** treated or heavily damped. No reverb tail. The mix has near-silence under the key line at
1:05, so any room noise is exposed there.

**Mic distance:** consistent throughout, with one deliberate exception — move slightly closer for
*"while there's still time to make decisions"* at 1:05.5.

**Also record, at the same session:**

1. **A slower slate of the full script** at ~145 wpm. If the natural read sits slower than 150, we
   pull the extra 2 seconds from the pause budget instead of squeezing the delivery.
2. **Three alternate takes** of the four KPI lines (0:32.9–0:41) at different spacings — the picture
   holds on one continuous dashboard shot there, so the edit needs room to breathe.
3. **The name alone, five times**, clean, for consistency patching.
4. **Thirty seconds of room tone** for the gaps.
5. **The final two lines separately**, with a longer pause between them, in case the lockup needs
   more time to animate.
