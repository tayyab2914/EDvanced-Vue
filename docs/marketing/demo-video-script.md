# EDvanced Vue — Demo Video Production Script

**Narration:** the client's "Narrator-Ready Demo Script", used **verbatim**. Not a word is changed.
**This document adds:** the visuals, the timing, and the capture list needed to shoot it.
**Runtime:** 1:29.4 of narration inside a 1:30 master.
**Tone:** conversational, confident, professional. Moderate pace, visuals allowed to breathe.
**Format:** motion graphics over real product screen recordings.

---

## 1. Can we shoot this as written? Yes.

Every screen the narration names is built and populated in the current milestone. Nothing in the
script needs a feature that does not exist, and nothing needs to be faked in After Effects:

| The narration says | The screen that shows it | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "a snapshot of your district's financial health" | `/dashboard` — Executive Dashboard, six KPI tiles | Built |
| "Projected fund balance" | KPI tile — literally reads **"Projected Unassigned Fund Balance"** | Built |
| "Days of operating cash" | KPI tile **"Days Cash on Hand"**; the Financial Health Summary row beneath reads **"Days of operating cash"** | Built |
| "Revenues and expenditures" | KPI tiles **"Revenues (YTD)"** and **"Expenditures (YTD)"** | Built |
| "areas that may need your attention" | **"Alerts"** KPI tile with a live count → `/alerts` | Built |
| "where your revenues are coming from" | `/revenues` → **"Revenues by Major Source"** | Built |
| "compare budget to actuals" | `/revenues` → **"Revenues vs. Budget (YTD)"** | Built |
| "identify significant variances" | `/revenues` → **"Top negative variances"** / **"Top positive variances"** | Built |
| "monitor how things are trending" | `/revenues` → **"Revenue Variance Trend"** | Built |
| "expenditures, fund balance, and cash position" | `/expenditures`, `/fund-balance`, `/cash` | Built |
| "monitor where you're headed" | `/fund-balance/forecast` — multi-year projection against the policy target | Built |

**One happy accident worth using.** The narration's four-part revenue sentence maps one-to-one onto
four cards that already sit on the Revenue Dashboard, in that order. We do not cut between four
screens for that line — we hold on one screen and let each card highlight as it is named. That is a
stronger shot than any edit we could build, and it is only possible because the page was designed
around the same four questions the narrator is asking.

## 2. Timing plan

Narration is **200 words**. Read at **150 wpm** with the marked pauses honoured, it lands at
**1:29.4** — inside a 90-second master with a music tail.

- Speech: 81.3s
- Marked pauses and breaths: 8.1s
- **Total: 89.4s**

> **Pace warning for the VO session.** At 145 wpm the same read lands at ~1:32. If the narrator
> naturally sits slower, take the 2 seconds out of the pause budget — not out of the KPI sequence
> at 0:32–0:41, which needs its beats to work. Record a slate at both paces and pick in the edit.

---

## 3. Shot-by-shot

Timecodes are the **narration** track. Visuals lead the audio by roughly half a second throughout —
the picture arrives, then the words describe it, never the reverse.

---

### 0:00–0:03 · Open

| | |
|---|---|
| **VO** | "Welcome to EDvanced Vue." *[Brief pause]* |
| **Visual** | White. The EDvanced Vue mark draws on — the split navy/green ring, the monitor, the bar chart rising last. Held clean. No UI yet. |
| **Motion** | Ring draws clockwise over 0.8s; bars grow from baseline, staggered. Settle, then hold through the pause. |
| **Audio** | Music enters soft and single-layer on the first frame. |

---

### 0:03–0:14 · The problem

| | |
|---|---|
| **VO** | "School finance teams have plenty of reports... and plenty of data. But the challenge is being able to quickly see what that data is telling you." |
| **Visual** | The logo lifts away. Reports and spreadsheet pages fan into frame — a dozen, then thirty — overlapping, slightly misaligned, all in cool grey. On "plenty of data" they multiply again and the camera drifts back to reveal there is no end to them. On "quickly see," everything slows and the frame holds still, unresolved. |
| **Motion** | Pages enter on a 40ms stagger. Deliberately *not* satisfying — the composition should feel slightly unresolved, because the sentence is describing a problem. Resist the urge to make this shot beautiful. |
| **Note** | Use real-looking financial tables, not lorem grids. Numbers should be legible enough to read as school finance, blurred enough to carry no meaning — that is the point of the shot. |

---

### 0:15–0:27 · The origin *[Pause — dashboard begins to appear]*

| | |
|---|---|
| **VO** | "EDvanced Vue was created by a school finance professional with that challenge in mind — helping finance teams turn the data they already have into information they can use." |
| **Visual** | During the pause, the grey pages begin to **organise** — they slide, align, and dissolve into the structure of the Executive Dashboard building behind them. The sidebar wipes in from the left. Cards land into the grid, empty at first. By "information they can use," the layout is fully formed but the figures have not populated yet. |
| **Motion** | This is the hinge of the whole video: chaos resolving into structure. One continuous move, no cut. Cards land on a 70ms stagger with a 12px rise and fade — match the app's own entry animation exactly. |
| **On-screen** | *(none — the narration is doing the work here; let the build carry it)* |
| **Audio** | Second music layer enters as the sidebar lands. |

---

### 0:27–0:33 · The snapshot

| | |
|---|---|
| **VO** | "When you log in, you immediately get a snapshot of your district's financial health." |
| **Screen** | `/dashboard` — Executive Dashboard, top of scroll. |
| **Visual** | The figures populate. All six KPI values count up simultaneously; status badges pop last with a 200ms delay so the eye lands on **status**, not just size. The "Data as of:" line fades up on the right. |
| **Motion** | Slow 3% push-in across the KPI row, running under the whole next section. Never let this shot sit perfectly static. |

---

### 0:33–0:41 · The four things that matter

The KPI tiles are named one at a time. **Do not cut.** Hold the full dashboard and let each named
tile lift 4px, brighten to full, and drop a soft shadow while the other five sit back at 45%
opacity. Each hand-off is a 250ms crossfade of emphasis, not an edit.

| VO | Tile that lifts | On-screen |
|---|---|---|
| "Projected fund balance." | **Projected Unassigned Fund Balance** — value, status badge, and *"Target ≥ X%"* sub-line all visible | *(tile speaks for itself)* |
| "Days of operating cash." | **Days Cash on Hand** — with its *"Policy ≥ X Days"* sub-line | *(none)* |
| "Revenues and expenditures." | **Revenues (YTD)** and **Expenditures (YTD)** lift *together*, as a pair | *(none)* |
| "And, most importantly, areas that may need your attention." | **Alerts** — the count, and its critical/warning note | Alert count pulses once; *"View All Alerts →"* underlines |

> **The most important 8 seconds in the video.** The narration escalates to "most importantly," so
> the visual must escalate with it. On the final line the other five tiles dim further, to 30%, and
> the Alerts tile holds alone. If the client's demo district has a critical alert, this is where its
> red badge earns its place — that single red chip is the argument for the whole product.

### 0:45–0:55 · The deeper look

| | |
|---|---|
| **VO** | "See where your revenues are coming from, compare budget to actuals, identify significant variances, and monitor how things are trending throughout the year." |
| **Screen** | `/revenues` — Revenue Dashboard, single continuous shot. |
| **Visual** | Four cards, already on the page, highlight in the order they are named. Camera makes a slow diagonal drift across the page rather than cutting. |

| VO phrase | Card that highlights | What animates |
|---|---|---|
| "where your revenues are coming from" | **Revenues by Major Source** | Rows fill top-down; the largest source's bar extends |

> threshold drawn on the chart, which is the difference between a trend line and a trend line that
> tells you something.

### 0:55–1:00 · The other three domains

| | |
|---|---|
| **VO** | "The same goes for expenditures, fund balance, and cash position." |
| **Visual** | Three fast beats, ~1.3s each, on directional whip-pans. Each: the dashboard's signature card fills frame, its chart draws, done. |

### 1:00–1:12 · The key message

| | |
|---|---|
| **VO** | "And instead of waiting until year-end to see where you landed... *[slow]* EDvanced Vue helps you monitor where you're headed — while there's still time to make decisions." |
| **Screen** | `/fund-balance/forecast` |
| **Visual** | On "waiting until year-end," a timeline sweeps forward and stops hard at a year-end marker — a dead end, greyed. On the ellipsis, the frame holds. Then on "where you're headed," the forecast screen takes over: the projection line **draws forward past today's marker** into the future years, and the dashed policy target line fades up beneath it. Where the projection crosses below target, the intersection flashes amber and a marker pins it. |
| **Motion** | The whole video's argument in one move: **backward-looking stops; forward-looking keeps drawing.** Slow everything down here — this is the client's marked "key message." The projection line should take a full 2.5s to draw. |
| **On-screen** | Small, restrained, near the crossing point: **While there's still time.** |
| **Audio** | Music thins to almost nothing under "while there's still time to make decisions." Let the line sit in near-silence. |

### 1:12–1:16 · The return

| | |
|---|---|
| **VO** | *[Pause]* "Because the numbers are only part of the story." |
| **Visual** | Pull back from the forecast to the Executive Dashboard, re-composing as one full clean view. As the camera settles, the **figures on the tiles soften and de-emphasise** while the status badges and the alert count stay at full strength — the numbers literally receding, the *meaning* staying. |
| **Motion** | Subtle. If a viewer consciously notices this effect, it is too strong. It should register as a feeling that the page is telling them something. |

### 1:16–1:25 · The close

| | |
|---|---|
| **VO** | "EDvanced Vue helps you see what's happening, understand what needs your attention, and tell the financial story behind the numbers." |

| **Beat 2** | "understand what needs your attention" → the Alerts panel slides over, one alert sentence legible |

## 4. Capture list

Sign in to the demo district — it is deliberately seeded in **mild difficulty** (reserve just under
target, days-cash under policy, budget utilisation past its warning), so status badges and alerts
are populated and real. A demo where nothing fires demonstrates nothing.

Record at 2560×1440 or larger, 60fps, then scale down — never up.

| # | Route | Capture | Needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `/dashboard` | Full page, top of scroll, all six KPI tiles + badges. **The hero asset — shoot this several times.** | 15s |
| 2 | `/dashboard` | Slow scroll to the Financial Health Summary (for the "days of operating cash" alternate, §5) | 6s |
| 3 | `/revenues` | Full page holding all four named cards in one frame | 12s |
| 4 | `/expenditures` | Spend-by-object breakdown card | 5s |
| 5 | `/fund-balance` | Unassigned fund balance trend against the policy target | 5s |
| 6 | `/cash` | Cash balance trend + days-cash health card | 5s |
| 7 | `/fund-balance/forecast` | Assumptions panel + projection chart, ideally crossing the target line | 12s |
| 8 | `/alerts` | Alert Overview with at least one critical and one warning | 8s |
| 9 | Sidebar | Cursor moving to and clicking **Revenues** (for 0:42) | 4s |
| 10 | `/login` | Brand lockup (optional, for the open) | 3s |

**Rules for the editor**

- Never hold on an N/A tile or an em-dash. The product deliberately shows an em-dash where a figure
  cannot yet be computed — correct behaviour, but out of context on video it reads as a bug.
- Do not retype numbers in After Effects. Recapture the screen. Every figure on screen must trace to
  real committed data, which is the same promise the product itself makes.
- Keep one district and one reporting period throughout. A figure that changes between two shots for
  no visible reason is the fastest way to lose a CFO's trust.

---

## 5. Four things to confirm before the shoot

1. **Which district's data.** The seeded demo district works and is safely fictional. If the client
   would rather show a real district's shape, we need a sanitised copy — names removed, figures
   scaled — before anything is recorded.

2. **"Days of operating cash."** The KPI tile reads **"Days Cash on Hand"**; the Financial Health
   Summary row directly beneath reads **"Days of operating cash"** — the narrator's exact words.
   Two options: hold on the tile (keeps the KPI row unbroken, minor wording mismatch), or dip to the
   health summary row for 1.5s (words match exactly, costs the continuity of the KPI sequence).
   **Recommendation: hold on the tile.** The sequence is stronger unbroken, and no viewer will
   notice. Shot #2 in the capture list covers the alternate if the client disagrees.

3. **The closing URL and CTA.** The script ends on the logo and tagline. Confirm whether a URL and
   "Request a demo" go on the final card, or whether it stays clean.

4. **Deliverables.** Proposed: 16:9 master at 1920×1080, a 9:16 vertical cut for social, a version
   with burned-in captions for muted autoplay, and a music-only version with no VO for use in live
   presentations where the client narrates in person. Confirm the list.

## 6. Production sequence

| Stage | Output | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Capture list shot, raw recordings reviewed | Demo data confirmed (§5.1) |
| 2 | VO recorded, two paces slated | Script locked (it is — narration is final) |
| 3 | Animatic — stills + VO, correct timing, no polish | 1, 2 |
| 4 | **Client review of the animatic** | 3 |
| 5 | Full motion pass, sound design, grade | 4 |
| 6 | Master + cut-downs delivered | 5 |

Stage 4 is the review that matters. Timing and shot choice are cheap to change on an animatic and
expensive to change after the motion pass — so that is the point at which to gather every note.
